December squeezes gig workers from both sides: spending goes up just as regular clients slow down. This is the story of how one freelancer filled the gap — two short holiday projects, a contract signed the same day, an invoice sent the moment the work landed. Her December, move by move

Week one — she went looking before she needed to. Instead of waiting for referrals, she opened a curated board of remote and flexible work and pitched for short, deadline-shaped projects: the kind businesses need finished before the holidays. Two said yes.

The same day — paper before work. Each project went into her client tracker with its dates and next step, and the agreement went out for signature before she did a single hour. No signed paper, no start — that one rule is what makes December income predictable instead of hopeful.

Delivery week — invoice on landing. She delivered early and sent the branded invoice the same day, while the work was fresh and the client's budget still had its holiday line open.

The evenings — a second, schedulable stream. A few online tutoring hours filled the gaps between projects — booked in advance, paid on a schedule, no chasing.

The result she aimed for: the gifts under her tree were bought with money that had already arrived — not with a card she would meet again in January.
